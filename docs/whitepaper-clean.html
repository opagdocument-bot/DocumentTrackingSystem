Executive summary

The Office of the Provincial Agriculturist processes several hundred documents a month across eleven programs. Every one of them is tracked on paper, in logbooks, by hand. When a Program Head asks "where is my Purchase Request?", the honest answer today requires walking to the Records desk, then to the Liaison, then possibly to the Provincial Budget Office.

SUBAYBAY — working name, from pagsubaybay, "to track" — is a two-part system: a web application for the office, and an Android application for the Liaison Officers who physically carry documents between the OPA and the rest of the Provincial Capitol.

The system does three things, in order of importance:

  1. It knows where every document is. Each document gets a control number and a QR code at intake. Every hand-off — OPA to Budget, Budget to Accounting, Accounting to the Governor's Office — is scanned, timestamped, and attributed to a named person.
  2. It keeps a digital copy. The Liaison photographs or scans the document at intake and at each signed stage. The result is a searchable digital library that survives staff turnover, typhoons, and misfiled folders.
  3. It shows Program Heads their own documents. A Rice Program coordinator opens a dashboard and sees the eleven documents currently out, who is holding each, how many working days it has been sitting there, and which ones have breached the service standard.

Critically, the system tracks the paper — it does not replace it. COA still audits original signed documents. SUBAYBAY is a register and a mirror, not a substitute. This is what makes it deployable in months rather than years, and what keeps it out of the legal thicket around electronic signatures on government disbursements.

2.1 What we are not solving

It is worth being explicit, because scope creep is the main way projects like this die. SUBAYBAY is not an accounting system, not a procurement system, not a payroll system, and not a replacement for the Provincial Government's financial systems. It does not generate the forms. It tracks documents that already exist, wherever they came from.

2.2 There is no incumbent

The Provincial Capitol currently has no document tracking system of any kind. That is worth pausing on, because it changes the project's position in three ways.

  • Nothing to integrate with, and nothing to fight. No existing system's data model to accommodate, no vendor to negotiate with, no competing internal project whose sponsor feels undermined. The most common source of delay in LGU software simply is not present here.
  • No comparison to lose. The system is measured against paper logbooks, not against someone else's software. That is a very forgiving bar for a v1.
  • This office is setting the provincial precedent. If SUBAYBAY works, it is the obvious template when the Capitol eventually wants one — and "eventually" tends to arrive right after an audit observation about document traceability.

That third point argues for one small, cheap hedge and nothing more. Keep the schema office-aware from the start: control numbers are already prefixed by originating office (OPA-RICE-PR-2026-00147), and offices already distinguishes internal divisions from Capitol offices. Adding owner_office_id to the programs table (§12.1) costs one column today and saves rewriting every row-level security policy later.

Resist anything beyond that. Do not build multi-office features nobody has asked for, do not add a tenancy layer, and do not let the possibility of a provincial rollout expand v1 by a single screen. Design so that expansion is possible; build only what this office needs.

Finding 1 — Half your volume is not in the procurement chain

OB Slips and Incoming Letters alone are 48% of everything the office logs. Purchase Requests are 6%. Any plan that starts with procurement is starting with a small fraction of the actual workload — worth knowing before committing the first three months to it. It also means the highest-value target for the "digital library" is incoming correspondence, not finance.

Finding 2 — The procurement chain goes dark after the Purchase Request

151 Purchase Requests are logged. Obligation Requests, across all three sheets that track them, total ten. Acceptance and Inspection Reports total two. The office records a PR going out and then loses sight of it until something comes back — which is precisely the gap that produces your one-to-two-month cycle and your near-universal deficiency rate. The tracking problem is not that the logbook is on paper. It is that the logbook stops at the office door.

Finding 3 — roughly 178 Post-Travel Reports are missing confirmed

222 Travel Orders. 44 Post-Travel Reports. And you have confirmed (Q-D15) that a Post-Travel Report is required for every travel.

That is an audit exposure of about 178 travels with no report on file, sitting in a spreadsheet where nobody can see it — not through anyone's negligence, but because no logbook can join two sheets and tell you what is missing from one of them. It is invisible by construction.

This is the clearest possible demonstration of what the system is for, and it is why the Travel Order chain is the right first build (§15.1). The gap is not something the system has to be extended to catch: it falls out automatically the moment both documents live in one registry with a link between them. A dashboard tile reading Travel completed, TEV outstanding produces a named, actionable list in the pilot's first week.

Finding 4 — The spreadsheet is drifting, as spreadsheets do

Three sheets track obligation requests. Two sheets named "Outgoing" have different column structures — one of them is a copy of the Incoming Letter sheet with its headings unchanged. Several sheets still carry the title of the sheet they were duplicated from: the tab named "Travel Order 2026" has "OB SLIP OPAg 2026" in cell A1, and "Purchase Request 2026" says "PURCHASE ORDER 2026". None of this is carelessness — it is what happens to any spreadsheet maintained by hand under time pressure, and it is the strongest practical argument for the system. A database has one definition of a document type, and copying a row cannot silently mislabel it.

3.1 The two numbers that reframe this project

From your answersQ-C3 · Q-C7

"Almost every document gets returned with a deficiency." And a Purchase Request or Disbursement Voucher takes one to two months end to end.

Those two facts are almost certainly the same fact. A document returned for a missing attachment does not lose a day — it loses a full trip to a separate building, a queue position, and however long it sits before anyone notices it came back. If the return rate is near universal, then most of your one-to-two-month cycle is rework, not processing.

This changes what the highest-value feature is. Tracking tells you where a document is; it does not stop it going out incomplete. The per-type attachment checklist in §7.3 is the feature that attacks the actual problem — a reviewer ticking off COA's documentary requirements before release, and the system refusing to mark a document ready while a required item is unticked.

I have moved it accordingly: the checklist is now a Phase 1 requirement rather than a refinement, and "% of documents returned for deficiency" belongs on the Office Head's dashboard beside the aging report. If the pilot halves that rate, it will have paid for itself regardless of anything else the system does.

One measurement task remains for Phase 0, and it is small: for two weeks, have the Records Section note why each returned document came back. Twenty entries would be enough to rank the deficiencies, and the top three almost certainly account for most of them. That list becomes the first checklist, and it is the difference between a checklist that reflects COA's general requirements and one that reflects what your office actually gets caught on.

Scope

In scope — v1
OPA documents only. The Travel Order chain first (TO → Itinerary → CTC → Post-Travel Report), then procurement, then the remaining families in §7. Incoming and outgoing communications. Android Liaison app. Web app for OPA staff. A no-install receiver PWA that external clerks can use by scanning a QR.
In scope — v2
Named accounts for external receiving offices; OCR full-text search; SMS notifications; analytics and monthly ARTA-style reporting; iOS build if ever needed.
Out of scope
Any document not belonging to OPA. Electronic signatures on financial documents; form generation; integration with eNGAS/eBudget; municipal agriculture offices; citizen-facing portal.
Deliberately deferred
Workflow automation that forces a route. v1 records what happened; it does not block a human from doing something out of order. Enforcement comes only after the recorded data proves what the real routes are.

That last point deserves emphasis. Systems that enforce a rigid workflow on day one fail in LGUs, because the documented process and the actual process differ — and the actual process is usually right. v1 suggests the next step and warns when something is unusual, but always lets the user record reality.

9.3 The SLA clock

Each document type is classified simple, complex, or highly technical, mapping to the RA 11032 standards of 3, 7, and 20 working days. Two design details make this actually work:

  • Working days, computed properly. The system holds a holiday calendar covering national regular and special non-working days plus local Aurora holidays and any Capitol-specific suspensions. Without this, every aging figure is wrong.
  • The clock stops when the ball is not in our court. Dwell time is attributed per-office. If a DV sits at Accounting for nine days, that is Accounting's dwell time, and the bottleneck report says so — factually, per office, without editorialising.

Escalation is graduated: at 70% of the standard the coordinator sees an amber chip; at 100% the Office Head's dashboard flags it; past 150% it appears on a weekly overdue report. No automatic emails to other offices in v1 — that is a political decision for the Provincial Agriculturist, not a default.

11.2 Why not an on-premise server

A server at the Capitol is the instinctive choice for government, and it has one real advantage — documents never leave the province. It also carries costs that are consistently underestimated: the machine, a UPS sized for Aurora's outages, someone competent to administer PostgreSQL, a static IP or VPN so Liaison phones can reach it from the field, and a disaster recovery plan for a province on the typhoon belt. If the Capitol loses power, the Liaison in Dipaculao cannot sync. For an office-scale system, it is the expensive answer to a problem you do not have.

11.4 Running this at zero recurring cost

The system is small — one office, roughly forty users, a few hundred documents a month. That fits inside free tiers with room to spare, provided the storage question is handled deliberately.

AnsweredCan Google Drive be the database?

No — and the reason is the whole point of the system. Drive is file storage; Google Sheets is the nearest thing to a database it offers, and it fails here on one decisive count: anyone with edit access can rewrite history. A tracking system whose record of "who held this document on which date" can be quietly edited afterwards is worth less than the logbook it replaces, because at least the logbook shows erasures.

Three further problems, any one of which would be disqualifying on its own. There is no row-level security, so a Program Coordinator scoped to Rice would be able to read every confidential Disbursement Voucher in the sheet. There are no transactions, so a Liaison syncing a hand-off over a weak signal can leave a half-written record. And the Sheets API allows roughly 60 read requests per minute per user, which several Liaisons syncing a morning's work would exhaust.

But Drive is the right answer for the scans. It gives 15 GB free against Supabase's 1 GB, and scanned images are the only part of this system that grows quickly. Postgres holds the registry; Drive holds the pictures; the document_files table holds the Drive file ID, the SHA-256 hash, and the metadata. Drive becomes the filing cabinet, Postgres the logbook — which is exactly the division of labour the office already understands.

Build noteDo not use a service account

The obvious way to connect a server to Drive is a Google service account, and on a free Gmail account it does not work. A service account has no Drive storage quota of its own. WarningFree tier gap

Supabase's free tier includes no automated backups. Daily backups begin on the paid plan. This is the one genuine risk of the zero-cost path, and it is not acceptable to leave uncovered for a system of record.

The fix is a scheduled job — a GitHub Actions workflow on a nightly cron is free and sufficient — that runs pg_dump, encrypts the output, and writes it to a Drive folder that only the administrator can reach. Add the quarterly restore drill from §14.3. Budget an afternoon in Phase 4 to build it, and treat it as a Phase 7 exit criterion. If the office ever finds ₱1,500 a month, upgrading Supabase to Pro is the single best place to spend it.

The second free-tier caveat is milder: Supabase pauses a project after seven days with no activity. A system used daily never pauses, but the pilot should not sit idle over a long holiday without someone touching it.

Two requirements hold regardless of cost. The database region must be named in the privacy notice, and the Drive account holding the scans must be an office account, not a personal Gmail — a system of record cannot depend on the continued goodwill of whoever's account it was set up under. You have confirmed opagdocuments@gmail.com for this; register Supabase, Cloudflare, the Play Store, and GitHub under the same address so no part of the system is tied to an individual.

11.6 Language — English and Filipino

You chose both, which is the right call for an office where staff read official forms in English and talk about them in Filipino. Three rules keep a bilingual interface from becoming a maintenance burden.

Built in from Phase 1
Every string in a resource file from the first screen. Retrofitting internationalisation means touching every component twice — react-i18next on web, expo-localization on mobile, one shared translation catalogue.
Per-user, not per-install
Language is a column on the user record, so each person picks their own and it follows them across devices. Default to Filipino on mobile, English on web, and let people change it.
Official names stay English
"Disbursement Voucher" does not become "Boucher ng Pagbabayad". Your staff know it as a DV, COA knows it as a DV, and translating official form titles makes the system harder to use, not easier.

That third rule is the one that matters most, and the boundary is worth stating precisely. Document type names, form titles, office names, and account codes stay in English — they are proper nouns of the bureaucracy. Everything the system itself says gets translated: buttons, instructions, status labels, validation errors, notification text, empty states, confirmation dialogs. A Liaison should see "Nasa Biyahe" as a status but "Travel Order" as a document type, and that mix is how people actually speak in the office.

The database stores codes, never display text — IN_TRANSIT, not "In transit" — so a translation change never touches data. Table 9.1 carries the first pass of status labels, and document_type_fields.label_fil carries the per-type field labels. Both need a native review before Phase 3 seeding.

13.2 QR receipt, and the receiver PWA

Since v1 is OPA-only, no other office is obliged to install anything. The receiver PWA is how you get a real acknowledgement from them anyway, without asking for cooperation: a single web page, no account, no install. The receiving clerk points their own phone camera at the QR on the transmittal sheet, a page opens listing the fifteen documents being handed over, they type their name, and tap Confirm. Ten seconds, on a phone they already have.

That gives three receipt flows in descending order of integrity, and the system records which one was used so the difference is always visible:

  1. Receiver scans the QR themselves via the PWA. Their name, the timestamp, and their device are recorded independently of the Liaison. Strongest.
  2. Receiver signs on the Liaison's phone. Signature image captured against the transmittal. Good.
  3. Liaison photographs the stamped receiving copy and types the receiver's name. Weakest, but honest — and it is what happens today, so it must be supported.

The PWA is deliberately trivial to build — one page, one API call — and it is the cheapest possible path to the external participation deferred out of v1. If PBO and PACCO start using it voluntarily, that is the argument for v2 making itself.

13.3 Offline is the default assumption

Non-negotiableConnectivity

Aurora's geography makes this the most important technical requirement in the document. Casiguran, Dilasag, and Dinalungan are hours from Baler over a coastal road, and coverage is inconsistent. An app that requires a live connection to record a receipt will be abandoned within two weeks.

Every write is queued locally in SQLite with a client-generated UUID and an idempotency key, then synced when a connection appears. The UI never blocks on the network. Conflicts are resolved by occurred_at ordering on the server, and any genuine conflict — two people receiving the same document — surfaces as a flagged event for a human to resolve rather than being silently overwritten.

14

Security, privacy, and records integrity

14.1 Access control

  • Email and password with a strong policy, plus mandatory two-factor authentication for Administrator and Office Head accounts.
  • Row-level security policies in PostgreSQL as the authoritative rule set; the frontend merely reflects them.
  • Liaison accounts bound to a registered device; a new device requires administrator approval.
  • Sessions expire; mobile refresh tokens are revocable per device from the admin console when a phone is lost.
  • Every read of a confidential document is itself logged.

14.2 Personal data compartmentalisation

Design constraintRA 10173

The forms folder contains CSC Form 212 (Personal Data Sheet) and the SALN. These hold sensitive personal information — dates of birth, government ID numbers, family details, and complete financial disclosures. A breach involving them is a materially different event from a leaked Purchase Request, both legally and in consequence for your colleagues.

The recommendation is firm: track these documents, but never store their images. The registry records that employee X submitted a PDS on date Y and that it reached PHRMO on date Z. The document itself stays on paper in the 201 file. This is enforced in the schema by document_types.capture_profile = 'none', which stops the camera from opening at all — not by a memo that a busy encoder will work around.

Payrolls, DTRs, and leave applications sit in a middle tier: scannable, but visible only to Admin, the employee concerned, and the Office Head — never to program coordinators.

14.3 Records integrity

  • SHA-256 on every uploaded file at capture, verified on retrieval.
  • PDF/A archival rendition generated for every completed document, per RA 9470 expectations.
  • Hash-chained event log, with a scheduled job that verifies chain continuity and alerts on a break.
  • Nightly encrypted database backups with a documented, tested restore procedure — one live restore drill per quarter, recorded.
  • Retention and disposal driven by document_types.retention_years, with disposal requiring an authorised, logged action.

14.4 Location capture

Recording GPS coordinates at hand-off strengthens the audit trail, and it also tracks your staff's movements. That is a real trade-off, not a technicality. The proposal: capture location only at the moment a hand-off is recorded, never in the background; disclose it plainly in the privacy notice; make it configurable and default it to off until the Provincial Agriculturist and the staff have agreed. See Q-G6.
